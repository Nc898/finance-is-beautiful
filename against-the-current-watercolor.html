  • Amount = starting amount × (1 + return)years. Returns and inflation are effective annual rates, and fractional years use the same formula.
  • Buys today = amount ÷ (1 + inflation)years. That is what the money buys in today's dollars.
  • A boat's place on the river is ln(buys today ÷ starting amount), which is years × (ln(1 + return) − ln(1 + inflation)). It grows in a straight line with time, so each boat moves at a constant speed.
  • Distance on the river is a log scale of buying power: each post marks a doubling or a step of one and a half times. All three boats share one scale, which adjusts smoothly when you change inputs.
  • The current is inflation. The boat's rowing is its return. Money only makes headway when the return is higher than inflation, and cash never rows.
  • Constant hypothetical rates, with no taxes or fees. Real inflation and returns vary.
